# Pricing the Research Product

What companies pay for developer research, who publishes prices, and the one rate card worth
copying outright.

## The template: Linux Foundation Research

**LF publishes a complete, itemized rate card for selling exactly this kind of research.** It is
the single most useful document found in all of this research, and the structure is directly
copyable.

### Sponsoring a study LF is already running

| Tier | Price | Slots | Benefits |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Title** | **$50,000** | 1 | Front + back cover branding, landing page logo, foreword authorship, showcase box, survey review, early access, own collector link |
| **Leadership** | **$15,000** | 4 | Tier-2 cover branding, showcase box, survey review, early access, own collector link |
| **Innovation** | **$5,000** | 8 | Tier-2 cover branding, acknowledgment, survey review, early access, own collector link |

```
1 × $50,000  +  4 × $15,000  +  8 × $5,000  =  $150,000 per study, fully sold
```

**This is the syndicated model with published prices and a fixed slot structure.** Thirteen
sponsors on one research artifact. It is exactly the structure proposed in
[economics.md](economics.md), except LF has already proven it sells and published what it costs.

Note the benefits at every tier: **survey review** and **early access**. Sponsors get input into
the instrument and a head start on the findings — not editorial control over conclusions. That
is the line that keeps the research credible while still being worth paying for.

### Commissioning custom research

| | Tier A: Snapshot | Tier B: Strategic | Tier C: Global Deep-Dive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Questions | 15 | 30 | 50 |
| Target completes | **150** | 300 | 500+ |
| Cross-tab banners | 5 | 15 | 30+ |
| Insights & charts | 15 | 35 | 60+ |
| **Price** | **$25,000** | **$55,000** | **$95,000+** |

> **Read Tier A again: $25,000 for 150 completed responses.**
>
> That is *below* a 200-person cohort. At LF's own pricing logic, a study of 200 elite builders
> sits comfortably in the **$25K–$55K band before any premium for audience quality** — and this
> audience cannot be bought on a panel at any price.

The persistent worry that 200 people is too small a sample to sell research on is answered here,
with a published price, by an organization that does this professionally.

Qualitative tiers run **$25–35K** (4–6 SME interviews) up to **$95K+** (20–30 interviews).
Bespoke workshops and roundtables start at **$75K**.

### The unit economic LF publishes and nobody else does

Third-party panel completes cost **"typically $24 to $30 based on region and quality,"** passed
through with **no markup**.

That number is the floor argument. A generic developer complete costs $24–30 on the open market.
A complete from a selected, flown-in, longitudinally-tracked elite builder is not available on
any panel at any price — which is the entire basis for a premium.

Source: [LF Research 2026 Sponsorship Prospectus](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/hubfs/Research%20Reports/lf_research_prospectus_050826a.pdf)

## The four business models in this market

| Model | Price point | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Participant access** — you do the research | $36–$94 per completed response | User Interviews, Respondent, CleverX |
| **Syndicated + custom questions** | ~$300 (consumer) → ~$1,400 (B2B) **per question** | YouGov B2B Omnibus, Researchscape |
| **Managed research platform** | $20K–$100K/yr subscription | Wynter, DX, UserTesting, dscout |
| **Commissioned research published as marketing** | **$50K–$250K+** per study | Forrester TEI, IDC Business Value |

The fourth is the largest budget line and the least price-transparent. Critically, that money
sits with **product marketing, not research** — a study a company can *publish with a third-party
logo on it* is a fundamentally different and better-funded product than a study they merely read.

## The published anchors

Almost nobody in this market publishes prices. The handful who do are the positioning weapons.

### YouGov B2B Omnibus — the per-question anchor

| | |
|---|---|
| Entry fee | £400 (n=500) / £450 (n=1,000) / £500 (n=2,000) |
| **Per question** | **£900** at n=500, **£1,100** at n=1,000 ≈ **$1,150–$1,400** |
| IT decision-makers | Priced at the **same tier** as standard business samples |
| Turnaround | 4–5 days standard; **8–10 working days for decision-maker audiences** |

Against consumer omnibus at **~$300/question** (Researchscape: $2,995 for n=1,000 and 10
questions), the B2B premium is roughly **4x**.

**Elite student builders are rarer than generic IT decision-makers.** $2,500–$5,000 per custom
question is defensible on these comparables, and the pitch writes itself against YouGov's own
published card.

### Wynter — the best packaging model on display

| Tier | Price |
|---|---:|
| Pro | **$20,000/yr** = 20,000 credits |
| Elite | **$40,000/yr** = 27,000 credits + research advisor + 6 managed projects |
| Wynter Black | **$100,000/yr** = 70,000 credits |

**1 credit = $1.** A typical test runs ~$1,599. **Pay-as-you-go costs 50% more than subscribing.**
Price scales with audience seniority. Panel: 80,000+ professionals verified by LinkedIn and
corporate email.

Three things worth stealing: the **credit denomination** (prices audience difficulty per study
without renegotiating the contract), the **PAYG penalty** (drives committed annual spend), and
**turnaround as the headline feature** — see below.

### RedMonk — proof the business works at a small entry price

| Tier | Price |
|---|---:|
| Startup / Foundation (<100 employees) | **$7,500 / 12 months** |
| Enterprise | **from $20,000** |
| Patron | Custom |

Published clients include GitHub, Red Hat, VMware (Patron) and MongoDB, GitLab, Docker, Oracle,
Fastly, LaunchDarkly, Honeycomb (Enterprise).

RedMonk's entry point is roughly **10x cheaper than Gartner's median seat** and its enterprise
floor is **below Gartner's cheapest observed contract**. A credible, focused developer-audience
research firm sustains a real business at $7.5K–$20K entry pricing — with a client list most
startups would envy.

### The analyst benchmark, from real contract data

| Firm | Median annual contract | Range |
|---|---:|---|
| Gartner | **$75,300** | $27,020 – $299,266 |
| IDC | **$68,435** | $14,100 – $112,200 |
| Forrester | **$65,510** | $25,000 – $500,000+ |

Gartner analyst advisory runs **$3,000–$7,000 per hour**. Forrester custom research projects:
**$50,000–$250,000+**.

⚠️ The widely-repeated "$50–100K for a Forrester TEI" figure **could not be traced to a primary
source**. Vendr's $50–250K consulting band is consistent with it but is not TEI-specific. Don't
cite it as fact.

## Speed is the most underpriced axis

| Vendor | Turnaround |
|---|---|
| **Wynter** | **12–48 hours** |
| YouGov decision-maker omnibus | 8–10 working days |
| Forrester TEI | ~8–16 weeks (estimated) |
| **SlashData** | **3–6 months** — gated on a twice-yearly survey wave |

Wynter sustains $20K–$100K/year subscriptions substantially on a 12–48 hour promise.

A live event with 200 builders in one room has a structural speed advantage over every
wave-gated survey panel in this market. A sponsor question defined in July can have behavioral
data in August and a report in September. SlashData physically cannot do that — their custom
questions wait for the next fielding window.

**Sell the calendar, not just the cohort.**

## Transparent pricing is a differentiator, not a concession

Not public: SlashData (every page routes to "Let's talk"), Stack Overflow advertising, Forrester,
IDC, Gartner, CB Insights, PitchBook, Wakefield, MLH, and every major collegiate hackathon
prospectus.

Public: **RedMonk** and **Linux Foundation Research**. That's it.

In a category where the incumbent closest to this product hides its price behind a sales call,
publishing a clear rate card is a genuine competitive move — it removes the single biggest
friction in a first conversation with a buyer who has never heard of you.

## Two corrections to earlier assumptions

**SlashData is far smaller than assumed.** Not 1M+ developers — **30,000+ surveyed per year**,
minimum **12,500 per wave**, two waves annually. Their 47.2M developer figure is a *population
estimate* derived from GitHub and Stack Overflow account counts, not a sample.

**The broad developer-survey category is losing steam.** Stack Overflow's Developer Survey:
**90,000 (2023) → 65,437 (2024) → 49,000 (2025)**. JetBrains gives away raw data free under
CC BY-NC-SA. Evans Data is serving an expired TLS certificate.

The money is visibly moving toward **narrow, high-trust, sponsorship-funded research** — which is
precisely where this product sits. Broad panels are commoditizing; curated cohorts are not.

## What to actually charge

Synthesizing every published anchor:

```
Study sponsorship, copying LF's slot structure
  Title           $50,000     1 slot
  Leadership      $15,000     4 slots
  Innovation       $5,000     8 slots
                 ─────────
                 $150,000     per study, fully sold

Custom research (LF Tier A-B, 200 completes)      $25,000 – $55,000
Custom question on the shared study                $2,500 – $5,000
Annual research partnership (RedMonk/Wynter tier) $20,000 – $100,000
```

The slot structure is the important part. It sells **thirteen sponsors one artifact** instead of
selling eleven sponsors eleven bespoke things — which is the only version of this a two-person
team can actually deliver.

# The Trap: $10K Is the Price of Student Access, Period

The single most important corrective in this research. **The ~$10K ceiling is not a hackathon
artifact.** It is what elite student access costs across the entire market.

| What | Price | What it includes |
|---|---:|---|
| **CMU Student Career Sponsor — Tartan** | **$10,000** | **Two academic semesters.** All CPDC career fairs, exclusive pre-fair info sessions and interviews, **4 resume books yearly**, direct connection to 4 student orgs, employer-in-residence, Employer Advisory Board seat |
| CMU — Scotty | $5,000 | Same structure, lower tier |
| **Stanford Computer Forum** | **$27,000/yr** | Recruiting access to **1,000+ Stanford CS/EE students**, on-campus interviews, info sessions, career fair, job board, **plus the right to place a visiting scholar inside a faculty research group** |
| CNCF gRPConf (200 attendees) | $10,000 | Supporter tier |
| CNCF Open Observability Summit (200+) | $5,000–$25,000 | Full ladder |
| KeycloakCon Japan (100 attendees) | $3,000–$10,000 | Full ladder |

Sources: [CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/career/employers/become-a-student-career-sponsor.html) ·
[Stanford Computer Forum](https://forum.stanford.edu/about/how-join-forum) ·
[CNCF prospectus](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/sponsor-cncf-2025_050125.pdf)

> **Stanford sells research *and* recruiting access to 1,000+ CS/EE students — including placing
> your researcher inside a faculty lab — for $27,000 a year.**

That is the number a sophisticated sponsor will reach for. Any pitch anchored on "access to
elite students" or "a 200-person technical event" lands in the **$5K–$27K band**, no matter how
good the students are. CNCF's own 200-person events price at exactly $5K–$25K.

**The strategic consequence is absolute: never let a sponsor frame this as student access.** The
moment the comparison is Stanford Computer Forum, the ceiling is $27,000 and the conversation is
over.

## What actually clears $50K

Every comparable above $50K is defined by a **research deliverable**, not by audience size — and
several are priced at *lower* participant counts than 200.

| Comparable | Price | Participants |
|---|---:|---|
| LF Research — Tier A commissioned study | $25,000 | **150 completes** |
| LF Research — Tier C deep-dive | $95,000+ | 500+ |
| LF Research — study Title sponsorship | $50,000 | avg **500 report downloads** |
| LF Research — bespoke roundtable | **from $75,000** | a room |
| Wynter — annual insight subscription | $40,000–$100,000 | **no event at all** |

Note the LF Title tier: **$50,000 for a study whose average download count is 500.** Buyers are
demonstrably not paying for reach.

Look at what the Title tier actually includes — **only three of nine benefits are branding.**
The rest are research rights: review the survey instrument before fielding, receive the raw
dataset, see findings before publication, author the foreword.

> **Pricing power lives in instrument-design rights, the dataset, and early access — not in the
> room.**

Named buyers of sponsored LF studies: **AWS, Meta, Intel**, Canonical, CNCF, Futurewei.

## Two defensible floors

Numbers a sponsor can verify themselves, which is what makes them useful.

### Floor 1 — panel replacement cost: ~$59,400

Buying 200 one-hour moderated sessions with technical B2B participants at published rates:

| Component | Published rate |
|---|---:|
| User Interviews recruiting fee, B2B + advanced targeting | $98 + $49 = **$147** |
| Incentive — Software Developer / IT Consultant (Respondent's published table) | **$150** |
| **Per participant, per session** | **≈ $297** |
| **× 200 participants** | **≈ $59,400** |

And that buys *one session each* — no cohort, no repeat access, no observation of anyone
actually building, no longitudinal follow-up.

Two structural facts worth using in a pitch:

- **Every platform charges exactly 2x for B2B over consumer** (User Interviews $98 vs $49;
  Respondent $80 vs $40). The professional-access premium is an established published
  convention, not a stretch you are inventing.
- **No platform publishes any rate for elite technical builders.** Prolific, Great Question,
  Centercode, Testlio, UserTesting and every expert network route it to sales. That opacity is
  itself evidence of undersupply.

### Floor 2 — cost per qualified developer lead: $33,600–$56,000

Events are the most expensive B2B lead channel on record: **$840 average CPL** (Sopro 2025,
range $180–$1,500+), with *qualified* leads at 3–5x reported CPL, and sales-qualified
software/IT leads benchmarked at **$1,680–$2,800** (Belkins 2026).

Twenty qualified contacts out of 200 elite builders = **$33,600–$56,000** of defensible value
before counting a single research output.

And the budget exists: **events already consume 41.1% of DevRel spend** — the largest single
line item — against Series A developer-marketing budgets of ~$200K and Series B+ of $1M+.
A $25K–$75K package is a normal slice, not an outlier ask.

## The market verdict, in two public filings

Put these in the deck. They are close to a direct answer to "why would this work?"

**Forrester (FY2025 10-K):**
> Events revenues **decreased 29%** during 2025... primarily due to a **decrease in sponsorship
> revenues**

And in February 2026 they announced they are **discontinuing strategy consulting while keeping
content-marketing consulting** — i.e. keeping the commissioned-research business and cutting the
other one. Contract value $292.4M across 1,797 clients ≈ **$162,700 average per client**.

**Gartner (FY2025 10-K):**
> Conferences revenues increased to **$644.7 million**... an increase of 11%... primarily due to
> an increase of **15% in exhibitor revenue**

53 conferences, 83,727 attendees → roughly **$12.2M per conference and ~$7,700 of revenue per
attendee**. Contract value $5.2B across 13,000+ enterprises ≈ **$400,000 average**.

> **Logo-placement sponsorship is contracting. Commissioned insight and high-yield-per-attendee
> formats are growing.** One research firm is exiting consulting-that-isn't-commissioned-research
> while its event sponsorship revenue falls 29%; another is monetizing conference attendees at
> $7,700 a head and growing on exhibitor revenue.

## Context: the large developer conference ladder

KubeCon + CloudNativeCon 2025 (the most transparent big developer conference):

| Tier | Price | Slots |
|---|---:|---:|
| Diamond | **$235,000** | 7 |
| Platinum | $144,000 | unlimited |
| Gold | $84,000 | unlimited |
| Silver | $29,500 | unlimited |
| Start-up / End User / Non-profit | $12,000 | unlimited |

Diamond rose **20.5% in a single year** ($195K → $235K). Non-members pay a flat **20%
surcharge**.

**Even at $235,000, the Diamond "data" benefit is an opt-in attendee registration list.** Not
structured research. The largest, most expensive developer conference on earth still sells
nothing but a mailing list at its top tier — which is the clearest possible statement of the gap.

## Revised pricing position

```
NEVER anchor here          $5,000 – $27,000     student/event access
                                                (CMU $10K, Stanford $27K, CNCF 200-person $5-25K)

Defensible floors         $33,600 – $59,400     panel replacement, qualified-lead value
                                                — numbers the sponsor can verify themselves

Target: research tiers    $25,000 – $95,000     LF's published commissioned-research ladder
                                                at participant counts at or below 200

Anchor / Title            $50,000 – $100,000    LF Title $50K; LF roundtable from $75K;
                                                Wynter enterprise $100K with no event at all
```

The earlier working target ($40–60K research tier, ~$100K anchor) survives this research — but
**only under the research framing.** Under an access framing the same event is worth $27,000,
and the sponsor has Stanford's published price to prove it.

Open every conversation with the deliverable. Never with the room.

## What a commissioned study can actually cost

From USAspending.gov, a single public federal award:

> **$2,800,000** — Dept. of Commerce, 1305M423FNEEA0111, **"CX STUDY"** — Forrester

One commissioned study. Not a subscription.

Forrester's contract value per client has climbed steadily: **$143K (YE2023) → $158K (YE2024) →

⚠️ Forrester **TEI study pricing remains genuinely unpublished.** No vendor has disclosed what
they paid. Treat any circulating "$50K for a TEI" figure as unverified; the sourced proxy is the
$50K–$250K+ consulting band.

## The floor argument, simplified

Published agency rates for **cost per completed survey response**, by how hard the audience is to
reach:

| Audience | Per complete |
|---|---:|
| General consumer | $2 – $5 |
| Niche consumer | $8 – $20 |
| B2B professionals (managers, directors) | $40 – $150 |
| **Hard-to-reach specialists** (physicians, engineers, C-suite) | **$200 – $500+** |

Sources: [Resourcera](https://resourcera.com/guide/market-research-cost/) ·
[Merren](https://merren.io/blogs/how-much-does-market-research-cost/) ·
[User Intuition](https://www.userintuition.ai/posts/market-research-cost/)

Note the incidence problem these guides cite: *"IT directors might have a 2–3% incidence rate."*
Screening for elite student builders is worse.

> **200 elite builders × $200–$500 per complete = $40,000–$100,000 in pure sample value —
> before a single question is written, before analysis, before the report.**

This is cleaner than the earlier $59,400 panel-replacement figure and rests on published agency
rate cards rather than a stacked calculation. Use it as the opening floor.

Context on total study cost from the same guides: basic quant B2B survey **$18K–$40K**; in-depth
quant **$40K–$100K+**; mixed-method B2B **$60K–$120K+**. Sample typically runs **~27% of total
project cost** — which, at $40–100K of sample value, implies a total study value well into six
figures.

## The three anchors to quote in a sponsor conversation

Each has a public URL the buyer can check themselves:

1. **$70,928/year** — one Gartner Technical Professionals team seat, for *generic* syndicated
   research (Florida DMS, published)
2. **$55,000** — a commissioned 300-complete study on a developer audience, base, before panel
   costs of $24–30/complete (Linux Foundation, published)
3. **$50,000** — top-tier co-branded research sponsorship *with foreword authorship* (Linux
   Foundation, published) — and that does not include recruiting access

Against those three, a **$50–95K research tier** for a study on 200 hand-selected elite builders,
instrumented in real time, with 30/90-day follow-up and recruiting access attached, is not an
aggressive ask. It is a discount on the third anchor with more delivered.

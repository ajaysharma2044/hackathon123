# Funding

Event 1 is the flagship: ~200 selected builders, flown in, housed, fed, 3 days.

## What it actually costs

Estimates, not quotes. Every line needs a real number before anything is committed.

| Line | Lean | Full | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Travel | $55K | $90K | 200 builders. Stipend-capped reimbursement, not booked flights. |
| Housing | $18K | $65K | 3 nights. Summer dorms vs. hotel — **the single biggest lever**. |
| Food | $30K | $48K | 9 meals + continuous snacks, 200 builders + ~30 staff/mentors/judges. |
| Venue | $0 | $45K | Target $0: sponsor office or university space, in-kind. |
| Prizes (cash) | $10K | $40K | Credits/hardware come free from sponsors; this is cash only. |
| Production / AV | $10K | $30K | Wifi capable of 250 concurrent devs is not optional and is often underbudgeted. |
| Swag / materials | $5K | $12K | |
| Insurance / legal / entity | $5K | $15K | Event liability, participant agreements, entity formation. |
| Staff / ops | $0 | $25K | Beyond the two of you. |
| Contingency (15%) | $20K | $50K | |
| **Total** | **~$155K** | **~$420K** | |

**Plan against ~$280K. Have a $165K version that still runs.**

### The four cost levers, in order of power

1. **Timing.** Mid-to-late August — after internships end, before semester starts. University
   dorms are empty and cheap, students are free, no exam conflicts. This alone can move housing
   from ~$65K to ~$18K. January intersession is the fallback.
2. **Location.** Ithaca is a travel-cost trap: small airport, expensive and awkward flights.
   NYC or Boston puts a large share of the target schools (MIT, Harvard, Columbia, Princeton,
   Penn, Yale, Brown, NYU, Cornell) within bus or train distance. Charter buses from 3–4 campus
   hubs; fly only Stanford, Berkeley, CMU, and Midwest.
3. **Stipend, don't book.** Cap travel reimbursement at a fixed number (~$300 domestic) and let
   participants book. Transfers the cost-control problem, removes change-fee exposure, and
   drops no-show losses dramatically.
4. **In-kind venue.** Worth $30–45K and, more importantly, is the first credibility signal —
   a sponsor hosting you is easier to reference in the next conversation than a sponsor paying you.

## Where the money comes from

### The stack

One realistic path to ~$280K:

| Tier | Count | Each | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Title / anchor research sponsor | 1 | $100K | $100K |
| Research sponsors | 2 | $40K | $80K |
| Recruiting sponsors | 3 | $20K | $60K |
| Activation sponsors | 5 | $8K | $40K |
| University / in-kind | — | — | venue, some housing |

Eleven sponsors. The anchor is the crux — it sets the price floor for everyone else and is the
reference that makes conversations 2 through 11 possible.

### The thing to understand about sponsor money

**Sponsors pay from different budgets, and those budgets have very different sizes and sales
cycles.**

| Budget | Owner | Size | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| University recruiting | Head of University/Early-Career Recruiting | Large, annual, already allocated | **Easiest first dollar** |
| DevRel / developer marketing | Head of DevRel, Developer Marketing | Moderate, discretionary | Moderate |
| Product research / insights | PM leadership, Research lead | Variable, often no line item | **Hardest to sell cold** |

The strategic consequence, and it is counterintuitive:

> **Lead with research. Close on recruiting.**

The research framing is what makes you different from every other hackathon and what earns the
meeting. But for most first sponsors, the money will come out of a recruiting budget, because
that budget already exists and is already spent on campus events with worse targeting. A single
senior engineering hire costs $30–50K in agency fees — that is the number your recruiting tier
is priced against, and it makes $20K for access to 200 elite builders an easy internal
justification.

Sell the research report as the reason they pick you. Let them expense it as recruiting.

### Pipeline math

Enterprise sponsorship converts at roughly 10–20% from first qualified conversation. Eleven
closes needs **60–100 qualified conversations**. At two people, that is most of one person's
capacity for six months. Budget for it as a job, not a task.

### Timing — work backward

Corporate budgets are set in Q4 for the following year, and university recruiting budgets track
the campus calendar. A late-August 2027 event means:

```
Oct–Dec 2026    anchor sponsor conversations (while next-year budgets are being set)
Jan–Mar 2027    close anchor; open the rest of the stack
Mar 2027        venue + housing contracts (go/no-go on cash in hand)
Apr 2027        applications open
Jun 2027        applications close, selection
Jul 2027        travel booking, logistics
Aug 2027        event
```

Starting anchor conversations after January 2027 means selling into budgets that are already
allocated. That is the deadline that actually matters.

### Non-sponsor sources — smaller, faster, real

- **Cornell**: eLab, Entrepreneurship at Cornell, Cornell Tech, department and college funds,
  student assembly. Typically $5–50K, and often venue in-kind, which is worth more.
- **VC sponsorship**: VCs pay for deal flow. Contrary, Z Fellows, Soma, General Catalyst, a16z
  and similar fund student builder events. $10–50K, and a fast decision cycle compared to
  corporates.
- **In-kind credits**: cloud and AI providers give credits readily. Funds the prize pool without
  touching cash.
- **MLH or a fiscal sponsor**: provides payment infrastructure and institutional legitimacy —
  and solves the problem of a company's procurement department being unable to cut a check to
  two individuals.

## Do not personally guarantee anything

Two students signing a hotel block or venue contract personally are personally liable for it.
A cancelled 200-person event with signed contracts is six figures of individual debt.

Before any contract is signed:

1. **Form an entity.** LLC at minimum, or operate under a fiscal sponsor / registered Cornell
   student organization that can hold contracts.
2. **Never sign a contract whose cancellation penalty exceeds cash already in hand.** This is
   the rule that keeps the downside survivable.
3. **Sequence commitments against cash.** Venue and housing are the large irreversible ones —
   they come after the anchor sponsor has paid, not after they have verbally agreed.
4. **Set a go/no-go date** with a defined funding threshold. Below the threshold, the event
   scales to the $165K version or postpones. Decide the threshold now, while it is abstract.

## The $165K version

If fundraising lands at half, this still runs and still produces the report:

- 120 builders instead of 200
- Regional: bus-reachable Northeast schools only, no cross-country flights
- 2 nights instead of 3
- University dorms, not hotel
- In-kind venue, mandatory
- Prize pool primarily credits and hardware

The research output survives all of these cuts. Panel size drops, which widens confidence
intervals — say so in the report rather than hiding it.

# Comparable Event Prospectuses

Real published ladders from events in this category. This is the most directly usable pricing
data found — these are the documents your prospects will compare yours against.

## Reality Hack at MIT — the closest analogue

Jan 22–26, 2026. A premium, curated, multi-day technical event. Their published ladder:

| Tier | Price |
|---|---:|
| Ecosystem: NETWORK | $3,000 |
| Tier 1: GROWTH | $7,500 |
| Tier 2: EXPANSION | $15,000 |
| Tier 3: ENTERPRISE | $30,000 |
| Tier 4: IMPACT | **$65,000** |
| Tier 5: MAXIMUM VISIBILITY | **$95,000** |

**They clear $65–95K — and they do it by bundling a conference and keynote into the top tiers.**
That is the proof a curated event can exceed the $30K hackathon ceiling, and it shows the
mechanism: add a format the sponsor can't get elsewhere.

### Their à-la-carte menu is worth copying almost wholesale

| | |
|---|---:|
| **Reality Scholars Fund — travel + accommodation for 4 hackers** | **$5,000** |
| Reality Scholars Fund, entry level | $500 |
| Founders Lab sponsorship (investor/scout-oriented) | $5,000–$15,000 |
| Prize benefactor (includes Demo Day judging seat + office hours) | $5,000 per prize |
| Conference marquee sponsorship + judging seat | $15,000 |
| Branded installation at the closing party | $15,000 |
| VIP dinner / meetup | $7,500+ |
| Sponsor a meal | $7,500 |
| Coffee break at your booth | $5,000 |
| Hydration station | $3,000 |

> **$5,000 buys travel and accommodation for four hackers.**
>
> That is your single largest cost line, sold as a named sponsor product. At 200 builders, 50
> scholarship units × $5,000 = **$250,000** — the entire travel and housing budget, packaged as
> something a sponsor wants to buy rather than something you have to fund.

Sponsors get named attribution on the builders they fly in. It is the best-feeling line item in
any prospectus in this category, and it directly attacks the 6–7x per-attendee cost problem.

Note their policy too: **prizes are billed separately and not included in the sponsorship cost.**

## Hack the North 2026 — Canada's largest, 1,000+ hackers

| Tier | Price |
|---|---:|
| Bronze | $8,000 |
| Silver | $16,000 |
| Gold | $30,000 |

The ladder mechanics are the instructive part — note how **recruiting access escalates**:

| | Bronze | Silver | Gold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rep passes | 4 → 8 | 12 | 16 |
| On-site recruiters | — | 1–2 | 3 |
| Resumes | post-event | **pre-event** | **pre-event + filtered by student interest** |
| Stage time | 1 min | — | 3 min |
| Name an area | — | — | ✅ |

"Resumes filtered by student interest" at the top tier is a small, smart idea: the scarce thing
isn't the resume, it's knowing who actually wants to talk to you.

Add-ons: sponsor an activity $3,500 · snack $3,000–5,000 · lightning challenge $2,500 · meetup
$3,500 · gamified quest integration $4,000.

## LIVE AI 2025–26 — multi-campus, sold explicitly as talent identification

Berkeley/Stanford/UCLA/UCSD, Duke/Harvard/MIT/NYU, Rice/SMU/Texas. 1,700+ participants,
60+ universities.

| Tier | Price |
|---|---:|
| Bronze | $1,000 |
| Silver | $5,000 |
| Gold | $10,000 |
| Platinum | $25,000 |

**20% discount for committing to all three events** — a multi-event commitment mechanic worth
stealing, since it converts one-off sponsorship into something closer to a subscription.

Platinum delivers participant emails + GitHubs, CVs + Devposts + LinkedIns, co-host status and a
keynote. They call it a **"talent identification program"** outright rather than a hackathon
sponsorship — a positioning choice, not an accident.

## Caltech Hacktech

**$1,000 / $2,000 / $5,000 / $10,000.** Recruiting ladder climbs from distributing materials →
receiving resumes → sending recruiters → onsite interviews → **an exclusive one-hour recruiting
session with Caltech students** at the top.

Their packet says plainly: **"ALL DETAILS ARE NEGOTIABLE."**

## Scarcity framing worth stealing

CSIhacks prices by **slot count**, not just tier:

| Silver $500 | 8 slots |
|---|---|
| **Gold $750** | **6 slots** |
| **Diamond $1,500** | **4 slots** |

Same mechanic as Linux Foundation's 1 Title / 4 Leadership / 8 Innovation. Scarcity does pricing
work that benefit lists cannot.

## The mechanism nobody else is using

**TreeHacks 2026 hosted a "Human Capital Fellowship" offering $50,000 equity-free per team
member — up to $200,000 per team.**

That is a fund buying deal access **through the prize pool** rather than through a sponsorship
tier. It sidesteps every equity and conflict problem in [venture-upside.md](venture-upside.md),
costs the organizer nothing, and is enormously attractive to participants. Worth considering as a
VC-facing product.

# The Business Model Corrective

## Do not charge the builders

| Program | Participant cost |
|---|---|
| South Park Commons | **$0** |
| Neo | **$0** — pays $20–40K |
| Interact | **$0** — fully sponsored retreats + travel stipends |
| Y Combinator | **$0** |
| Thiel Fellowship | **$0** — pays $250K over 2 years |
| buildspace | **$0** — sponsorship-funded |
| **On Deck** | **$2,990** |

**On Deck is the one that charged. It is also the one that collapsed.** Layoffs of 25% in May
2022, then a third of remaining staff in August. Its ODX accelerator fund targeted $100M and
closed **$23M**. Its accelerator terms fell from $125K/7% to $25K/1%.

The earlier note in [asset-monetization.md](asset-monetization.md) that Edge Esmeralda charges
$2,400–3,000 stands — but that is a residency for adults with income, not a competitive
recruiting event for students. **At the elite student end, the fee model is empirically the weak
one.**

## The two durable models

**1. The fund finances operations.** South Park Commons states it outright: *"the venture fund
finances operations."* Neo, Contrary, and Dorm Room Fund are the same shape. This is not
available to you in year one — and [venture-upside.md](venture-upside.md) explains why pairing
it with a research business creates the Carta problem.

**2. Hack Club's 7%.** The only non-fund model with verified nine-figure trajectory, from actual
IRS 990 filings:

| FY | Revenue | Expenses | Assets |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2019 | $1,648,010 | $556,873 | $1,203,719 |
| 2021 | $3,041,120 | $1,711,023 | $3,032,702 |
| 2023 | $11,357,856 | $6,018,628 | $10,313,634 |
| **2024** | **$22,351,707** | $14,214,400 | $18,742,497 |

The mechanic: **7% of incoming funds** on fiscal sponsorship, credit-card processing included, no
startup cost, HCB-to-HCB transfers free. 1,500 sponsored projects. Contributions were 95.4% of
2024 income.

A 7% skim on money *flowing through* a community scaled from $1.6M to $22.4M in five years.
Worth serious thought alongside the 501(c)(3) question in
[research-findings.md](research-findings.md).

## The honest sponsor math

Synthesizing every comparable event ladder above:

```
~200 elite builders, per corporate partner:     $8,000 – $30,000  typical
                                                $65,000 – $95,000  ceiling, with a
                                                                   conference/keynote bundled

10–15 partners × $15–30K  =  $200,000 – $450,000
```

**That range clears the ~$280K budget.** It is also a more grounded target than "11 sponsors at
$25K" — it comes from what comparable events actually charge and actually sell.

It does mean a **10–15 account pipeline**, not 70 like HackMIT. Still demanding for two people,
but achievable — and the research tier is what lifts the top of that range from $30K toward
Reality Hack's $65–95K.

For calibration: buildspace's founder pegged a sponsorship-only builder-community business at
**$3–5M ARR** — at a scale far larger than 200 people.

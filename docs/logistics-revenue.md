# Logistics Revenue

Research findings on the money hidden inside event costs. Sources at the bottom.

> **Scope note:** the room-block mechanics here — IATA TIDS, commissions, comp-room ratios,
> rebates, attrition clauses, meal and hotel-adjacent sponsorship — are industry-standard and
> apply in **any** city. Only the GMCVB incentive, the Florida hurricane program, and the Miami
> venue quotes are location-specific. Location is still open; see [location.md](location.md).

**Headline: a 600-room-night event can recover roughly $30,000–$45,000 from its own logistics** —
about $20–26K from the room block and the rest from meal and hotel-adjacent sponsorship. That is
more than a Cal Hacks Silver sponsor, from money most organizers never realize is on the table.

## Do these two things before sending a single RFP

### 1. Register for IATA TIDS. It is free.

> **"Hotels do not pay commissions to individuals. They pay commissions to agencies."** — Groups360

That sentence is the whole problem. Without an agency identifier, every dollar of room-block
commission is simply forfeited.

**IATA's Travel Industry Designator Service (TIDS) is free** and explicitly built for *"sales
intermediaries such as meeting planners and event organizers who are not currently
IATA-Accredited Agents."* IATA states suppliers use it to identify partners **including granting
commissions**.

Caveat: many, but not all, suppliers recognize TIDS. The alternatives are a host agency
(monthly/annual fees plus a commission split) or full IATAN accreditation (higher cost and
compliance).

This is the single highest-return administrative action available — free, fast, and it converts
"organizer who cannot be paid" into "intermediary who can."

### 2. Send the RFP through GMCVB, not direct to hotels

The Greater Miami CVB pays **$3.00–$5.00 per occupied and paid guest room** — but *"the initial
RFP must be sent to GMCVB directly for approval to qualify."* Book direct and you forfeit it
permanently.

At 600 room nights that is **$3,000**. Conditions:

| | |
|---|---|
| Travel dates | **June 1 – September 30** only (summer need period) |
| Minimum | 50–100 rooms/night, 3-night minimum stay |
| Booking deadline | New bookings before **Sept 30, 2026** |
| Actualization | Before Sept 30, 2027 |
| Payment | After the program, on a GMCVB-approved audited rooming list |

**Note the booking deadline is Sept 30, 2026 — about two weeks from now.** An August 2027 event
would qualify on the actualization window, but not on the booking window unless a contract were
signed almost immediately, which is not realistic with no funding in place. Call GMCVB
(Sales@GMCVB.com, 305-539-3000) and ask whether the program renews for the following cycle —
this incentive independently reinforces holding the event in the June–September window.

## Room block economics

| Lever | Real numbers |
|---|---|
| **Commission** | **7%** at large chain hotels, **10–12%** at independents. Industry range 5–15%. On a $200 rate, 7% = $14/room night. |
| **Comp rooms** | **1 per 50 paid room nights** is the true baseline. 1:40 common, 1:30–1:40 achievable for shoulder dates, high F&B spend, or repeat history. At 600 nights: **12 comps at 1:50, 15 at 1:40, 20 at 1:30.** |
| **Rebates** | **$5–$15 per room night** → **$3,000–$9,000** on 600 nights. Paid ~30 days after last departure. |
| **Attrition** | 80% pickup / 20% slippage is standard. No true industry standard — fully negotiable. |

### Three contract details that are worth real money

**Comps are earned on actual pickup, not contracted block size.** Underperform and you forfeit
them entirely. They also default to standard rooms only — applying them to suites or converting
them to a dollar credit **must be written into the contract**.

**Rebates reduce the commissionable base.** A $10 rebate plus 10% commission yields $34/night,
not $35. They do not stack cleanly.

**Negotiate attrition cumulatively, never per-night.** On a 200-room block at 20% attrition, the
same actual pickup produces a **10-room shortfall cumulative versus a 16-room shortfall
per-night**. Damages run at roughly shortfall × ADR × 80%. Also negotiate a resell credit and
exclusion of taxes and resort fees from damages.

**Rebates must be disclosed.** Undisclosed rebates *"could be considered a 'bribe' or other
impermissible payment"* under state commercial bribery law. Standard disclosure language: *"A
portion of the room rate will be used to offset Group's meeting expenses."*

## The room block is also sellable ad inventory

The most under-exploited finding. From a published rate card at a 600-attendee conference:

| | |
|---|---:|
| Custom hotel room keycards | **$10,000** |
| In-room TV welcome channel branding | **$10,000** |
| Room drop — branded gifts | **$2,500** |

None of these touch the room rate, and none of them exist at a normal hackathon because normal
hackathons have no room block. Housing 200 people creates sponsor inventory that a commuter
event cannot sell at any price.

## Food and beverage sponsorship

Real, but understand the mechanism: **it is expense recovery on branding, not brands paying to
feed people.** Buyers are overwhelmingly industry vendors buying signage on meals you still pay
for. PCMA notes F&B sponsorship investment *"has been declining for years"* and recommends
framing it explicitly as recovery — **$5,000 against a $10,000 coffee break** beats no sponsor.

Published comparables (600-attendee conference):

| | |
|---|---:|
| Boxed lunch (logo stickers) | $2,000 |
| Networking breakfast | $2,500 |
| Networking lunch | $3,000 |
| Exhibit-area breaks (2 days) | $14,000 |
| Evening reception | $15,000 |

**Scale caveat: at 200 attendees the realistic band is the $2,000–$3,000 meal tier.** The
$11–15K reception tier is not available at this size.

Rule of thumb: price sponsorships at **10–20% of what equivalent exposure would cost as paid
advertising**.

## Two firm negative findings

**Miami-Dade TDC grants are closed to you.** The FY2025-26 guidelines state verbatim: *"The TDC
does not provide funding for national and international conventions, trade shows and tourism
industry exhibitions, seminars and conferences."* The guidelines explicitly redirect conference
organizers to GMCVB. (Caps would have been $5K–$35K by budget size; reimbursement-only, ~6
months to payment.)

**American Airlines group fares structurally do not fit this event.** Their published terms
require that *"all passengers in the group must travel together on at least one common flight
segment"* with outbound travel commencing on the same date. Two hundred attendees flying from
different cities do not qualify. The right product is a **meeting fare** (25+ passengers on
different itineraries converging on one city), but current terms could not be verified — the
widely circulated "5–10% off, one comp ticket per 40 purchased" figures trace to **ATA Airlines,
which no longer exists**. Treat them as dated and unverified. Call AA Group & Meeting Travel at
800-433-7300.

Confirmed AA group terms if relevant later: 10-passenger minimum, block up to 11 months ahead,
deposits $100–$400/passenger by cabin, balance due 30 days out, all tickets nonrefundable, one
free name change per ticket.

## Stacked model — 600 room nights

⚠️ **Group ADR is a placeholder.** Miami group ADR could not be verified and is the one input
that must be replaced with a real quote.

At an assumed **$250 group ADR** (unverified), room revenue = $150,000:

| Stream | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Commission @ 10% (net of rebate) | 600 × $240 × 10% | $14,400 |
| Rebate @ $10/night | 600 × $10 | $6,000 |
| Comp rooms @ 1:50 | 12 × $250 | $3,000 (in-kind) |
| GMCVB incentive @ $5 | 600 × $5 | $3,000 |
| **Room block subtotal** | | **~$26,400** |

At 7% chain-hotel commission this drops to ~$20,000. Adding meal, break, and hotel-adjacent
sponsorship at 200-person scale brings the realistic all-in to **$30,000–$45,000**.

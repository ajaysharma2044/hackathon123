# Hackathon123 -- LLM Council Verdict (First Wedge & Riskiest Assumption)

**Related:** [[hackathon123 MOC]] · [[hackathon123-initial-context]]

## For future Claude

Advisory synthesis (not an owner decision) produced 2026-09-12 by running the LLM Council skill on the hackathon123 idea while it was at pure idea stage. The council was asked one thing: what should the first move be, and is a flown-in premium flagship event the right entry point? Five independent advisor lenses + an anonymized peer-review round converged hard on one answer -- **do not run an event first; pre-sell the sponsor research report and test whether a buyer will pay real cash before building anything.** This note captures the framed question, the verdict, and the caveats. It is decision-support, not a committed plan -- scoping remains owner-driven per [[hackathon123 MOC]].

---

## The framed question put to the council

Two-person side project, pure idea stage (no capital, name, or scope committed). The pitch: a premium hackathon for ~200 hand-selected elite student builders (MIT/Stanford/Berkeley/Ivy/CMU) -- flown in, housed, fed, mentored, given sponsor tools and prizes -- that doubles as a live R&D / experimentation / recruiting network underneath. Sponsors do not buy logos; they bring a real business question (e.g. "why do strong devs choose Claude over competitors, and what blocks adoption?"), the platform designs an experiment (required exposure -> free choice -> observe adoption/switching/retention), captures a consented behavioral event stream, tracks continuation at 7/30/90 days, and returns a research report. Long-run vision: an "experimentation marketplace." Claimed moat: compounding longitudinal data across many events.

**Decision:** what is the first move, and is "fly in 200 elite students for a premium flagship event" the right entry point at all? Three candidate wedges:

- **A. Event-first** -- raise/spend to run one genuinely elite flagship event, prove you can attract the panel and sponsors, generate the first dataset.
- **B. Research-product-first** -- sell the sponsor decision-intelligence product using a cheaper, smaller format before ever running a flown-in premium event.
- **C. Data/network-first** -- build the longitudinal behavior + work-evidence layer around builders who already exist (leaning on the partner's separate Club OS campus data), and treat the hackathon as one later capture format.

Constraint: no outside funding committed. Which wedge, and what is the single riskiest assumption to test first?

---

## The five advisor lenses (one-line thesis each)

- **Contrarian** -- you are selling research validity you cannot deliver (selection bias + Hawthorne + prize-contaminated "free choice" on n~=200); use B as a knife to kill the idea cheaply, not a launch plan.
- **First Principles** -- the atomic unit of value is a sponsor writing a check because a report changed a decision; the event is a data-collection apparatus in a costume; pre-sell, do not run anything.
- **Expansionist** -- the elite panel is a priceless, underpriced asset (research subscriptions, recruiting toll, VC deal-flow); wedge B lets the report fund the flagship rather than the reverse.
- **Outsider** -- "pick one noun" (hackathon producer? research firm? recruiter?); a company can already answer the flagship question with its own interviews + analytics; hand-picked elites are among the least representative humans alive.
- **Executor** -- wedge B, pre-sell version; build a sample report, pitch named dev-tool buyers this week, ask for a $5-15K paid pilot, kill in ~4 weeks if no signed check.

---

## Where the council agrees

Near-unanimous convergence, which is itself the strongest signal in this verdict:

- **All five independently chose Wedge B -- redefined as "pre-sell the report," not "run a smaller event."** You need neither an event nor existing data to sell the deliverable; research services sell on a credible pitch + sample.
- **Event-first (A) is rejected by everyone.** It spends the most money to test the *least* risky assumption ("can two capable people run a nice event?" -- money and prestige solve that) while deferring the one that actually matters.
- **The single riskiest assumption is the same across all five:** will a sophisticated buyer write a real check ($10K+) for a decision-grade answer about this population -- cash for a decision, not a logo -- when they already have their own telemetry? Everything collapses if the answer is no.
- **The event is the costume; the answer is the product.** The moat is a *consequence* of repeatedly selling answers, not something built up front.

## Where the council clashes

- **Priceless asset vs measurement artifact.** The Expansionist alone is bullish -- a consented longitudinal panel of elite builders is something DevRel/AI-labs/VCs are starving for. Every other advisor and all five reviewers flagged this as the council's biggest blind spot: it assumes the asset's value is proven when generalizability is the untested core. Honest resolution: the bull case is real *only if* the data generalizes and buyers believe it -- which is precisely the untested assumption. Upside is conditional on passing the bear's test.
- **Is "elite" load-bearing or vanity?** First Principles and the Outsider argue that if the report is the product, the sampling frame is whatever makes the answer credible to the buyer, not logo prestige. The Expansionist treats "elite" as the entire moat. Unresolved.
- **Can the product even be delivered well?** The Contrarian goes deeper than sequencing: the methodology (selection bias, Hawthorne, prize-contaminated free choice, n~=200) means "a competent DevRel lead shreds it in one meeting." This questions whether the product can ever be *good*, not just whether it sells.

## Blind spots the council caught (emerged in peer review)

The first was flagged by 4 of 5 reviewers independently -- the highest-confidence thing the individual advisors missed:

- **The entire council tested demand and ignored supply.** No advisor asked whether two unfunded people can *recruit and retain* 200 genuinely elite builders across 7/30/90 days once they know they are the tracked product. That retention is the actual engine of the compounding-data moat -- and it is harder than the first check. Cutting the prestige event to go lean may remove the very magnet that recruits the supply.
- **A signed check proves consulting, not the company.** One bespoke pilot validates one-off research demand; it does not test repeatability or whether data compounds across buyers -- which is the actual marketplace thesis. Treat the pilot as a gate, not vindication of the vision.
- **Sponsor-funded research has a built-in credibility discount.** The sponsor pays, and prizes nudge their own tool -- a conflict of interest that compounds the sampling critique.
- **(Minority, but sharp)** Recruiting / talent access may have higher and more proven willingness-to-pay than a report -- firms already pay $20K+ per elite hire. The easiest first check might be for access to the people, not a PDF.

## The recommendation

**Wedge B, the pre-sell version -- do not run an event.** It is the cheapest possible test of the one assumption everything rides on, backed by the strongest reasoning (the Executor's falsifiable test + First Principles' atomic-value logic + the Contrarian's "use B as a knife" framing).

Fold in the two peer-review corrections so the test is aimed correctly:

1. **Probe demand and offer-shape at once.** In the same sales calls, learn whether buyers would pay for a *report* or for *talent access* (the higher-WTP guess). Do not assume the report is the product.
2. **Be honest that one pilot only tests consulting demand,** not the marketplace/moat. Hold the Expansionist's vision as the prize, not the plan -- it is fundable only after the first check proves people pay.

And carry the Contrarian's warning into every pitch: have a ready answer for "why does this generalize / beat my own telemetry?" If you cannot answer it credibly, the sophisticated buyer says no -- and you have learned the core thesis is weak for the cost of a few conversations.

## The one thing to do first

**Build one polished one-page sample report** (mock data, real structure: funnel -> ranked friction points -> recommendation) for a single real buyer's actual question, and **book pitch calls this week with 5-10 named dev-tool companies fighting for student mindshare** (Cursor, Vercel, Replit, Anthropic, ...). Pitch the deliverable, not the event; ask for a paid pilot ($5-15K) on the cheapest format that yields a real behavioral stream. **Kill criterion: no signed paid commitment or LOI in ~4 weeks means the flown-in event would have flopped too.**

## Method & caveats

- Produced with the LLM Council skill (Karpathy-style): 5 independent advisor lenses answered in parallel, responses were anonymized (A-E) for a peer-review round, then de-anonymized for this chairman synthesis. All advisors were the same underlying model with different assigned thinking styles -- this is structured multi-perspective reasoning, not five different models or any external data.
- **Confidence: advisory.** Nothing here is fact-checked market research. No external sources were consulted; claims about buyer willingness-to-pay, per-hire economics, and competitor telemetry are advisor priors, not verified figures -- validate them in the actual sales calls the recommendation prescribes.
- This does not commit the project to Wedge B. Scoping remains owner-driven (and partner-shared) per [[hackathon123 MOC]].

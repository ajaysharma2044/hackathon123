## 1. Winning hackathon
**GridCompute: Build the Operating Layer for Power-Constrained AI.** Teams build and stress-test software, optimization and control strategies that co-optimize AI workloads, data-center power/cooling/storage and grid constraints in a shared digital twin. ## 2. Why Cornell wins
The real advantage is the intersection **ORIE optimization × ECE power systems × electricity-market design × AI/systems × hands-on project-team builders**. Current evidence includes ORIE 5235, E3RG/POWERWEB, Lang Tong's grid/optimization work, and Cornell Engineering's 36 project teams / 1,800+ students. Cornell is not uniquely capable in an absolute sense; its advantage is intersection density.

## 3. Economic battle
AI infrastructure is colliding with power-system constraints. FERC is forcing large-load integration reform; DOE says transmission need is rising with data centers; PJM reported a July 2026 event where nearly 4,000 MW of computational load unexpectedly disconnected; IEA projects data-center electricity demand roughly doubling by 2030. The expensive problem is how to schedule, power, cool, connect and ride through dynamic AI loads.

## 4. Why a hackathon
The event is useful where parallel search has value: workload schedulers, control architectures, robust optimization, failure discovery, integrations and prototype diversity. Internal engineering/consultants are better for implementation. The hackathon's structural edge is **max-of-N solution search + diversity + failure information**, not cheap labor.

## 5. Problem workflow
AI capacity need → power envelope/site → grid/facility constraints → workload/power/thermal forecast → workload scheduling → GPU/rack power policy → cooling/storage/backup coordination → disturbance/curtailment response → throughput/latency/reliability/cost measurement → policy update.

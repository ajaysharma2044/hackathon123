# Hackathon123 -- Sponsor-Pitch Benchmark Brief

## For future Claude / for the cofounder

A call-ready cheat-sheet of real, sourced numbers for sponsor conversations -- organized by pitch beat. Every figure is verified to a retrieved source (2026-09-14 run) with a confidence tag; full URLs + dates are in [[comparable-events-dataset]]. This exists so the cofounder can quote defensible numbers instead of hallucinated ones. Read the "Never quote" list at the bottom before any call.

> [!warning] Rules of use
> **Quote freely:** figures tagged **stated** or **high**. **Say "roughly / directionally":** **medium**. **Never state as fact:** anything in the "Do NOT quote" list -- those are vendor marketing or contradicted figures we deliberately excluded. When in doubt, the dataset note has the source URL -- open it before a high-stakes call.

## Beat 1 -- "The market for developer intelligence is real and funded" (open here)

- **Zoom acquired Common Room (2026-07-02)** for AI-native buyer/developer intelligence; customers included Anthropic, Atlassian, Notion, Okta, Snowflake. *(stated -- a public company buying this exact capability, not a rumor.)*
- **Reo.Dev raised an $11.3M Series A (2026-07-17, Elevation Capital)** on top of a $4M seed -- specifically for developer buying-signal intelligence; 100M+ engineer profiles, 200+ customers (NVIDIA, LangChain, ElevenLabs). *(stated.)*
- **SlashData** has sold syndicated developer research (30,000+ developers/yr across 168 countries) to Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Intel, and Meta for ~20 years. *(stated.)*
- **Gartner (2024, 632 B2B buyers): 61% prefer a fully rep-free buying experience** -- i.e., most of the buyer journey is invisible to vendors. *(stated -- use this, not the recycled "17%"/"70-80%" figures.)*

## Beat 2 -- "Vendors only see their own users; no one runs neutral longitudinal research on elite builders"

- The dark-funnel/rep-free problem above means companies **cannot see the developers who chose someone else.** A neutral party observing real tool choice across an elite panel is structurally positioned where no single vendor is.
- **Honest framing (do not oversell):** developer-intelligence products can get absorbed rather than thrive standalone -- **Orbit raised ~$19-21M and was acquired by Postman (2024), product shut down within 90 days.** The event + proprietary longitudinal panel is what makes this defensible where passive-signal plays were not. *(stated.)*

## Beat 3 -- "Here's what sponsors pay for comparable access" (pricing anchors)

- **Elite collegiate hackathon sponsorship: ~$10K-$50K.** Cal Hacks 2026: Bronze $10K -> Anchor $50K (recruiting perks gate at $20K+). *(stated, hackberkeley.org.)*
- **MIT Reality Hack: $3K -> $95K** (highest confirmed single hackathon-style tier). *(stated.)*
- **Developer-conference ceiling: CNCF/KubeCon Diamond $235K-$282K** at 10-12k attendees -- price scales with attendance. *(stated.)*
- **Caveat to state honestly:** premium AI-builder events (Cerebral Valley, AI Engineer, AGI House) keep sponsorship pricing **inquiry-only** -- no public rate card exists, so above ~$50K there is no public benchmark. *(stated absence.)*

## Beat 4 -- "Recruiting alone can pay for the sponsorship" (the cleanest ROI)

- **Cost-per-hire: ~$5,475 average (SHRM); ~$9,000 tech median; third-party recruiter ~20-25% of first-year salary (~$20K-$32K on a $100-130K hire).** *(medium -- cite as "industry benchmarks around.")*
- **Quant/HFT firms already pay to recruit elite student builders:** HackMIT 2025's 42+ sponsors were led by **Jane Street, Hudson River Trading, Citadel, and D.E. Shaw** alongside AI labs. *(stated.)*
- **Takeaway line:** "At a ~$20-32K recruiter fee per elite hire, a $10-50K research+recruiting sponsorship pays for itself on 1-4 hires -- and you also get the research." *(the arithmetic is sound; the per-event hire count for our event is unproven.)*

## Beat 5 -- "This cohort is genuinely elite" (selectivity proof)

- **TreeHacks 2026: ~6.7% acceptance (15,000 applications -> ~1,000 spots).** *(high.)*
- **Anthropic's own Opus 4.7 hackathon: ~2.5% (500 from 20,000+ applicants).** *(high.)*
- **Cerebral Valley Opus 4.6: ~3.8% (500 from 13,000).** *(stated.)*
- **Takeaway:** elite builder events run low-single-digit acceptance -- a ~200-person invite panel at that selectivity is a credibly elite cohort, comparable to the Cerebral Valley AI Summit tier (200-350, invite-only).

## Beat 6 -- "We can produce research that's credible and that you can't get elsewhere"

- **Post-event project survival baseline (Nolte et al. 2020, 78 hackathons): 35.3% of projects still committing right after the event, ~3.5-5% at 5 months; team skill diversity cuts abandonment 71%.** *(stated -- the honest baseline for our 7/30/90 retention product.)*
- **The flagship question -- "is AI actually making developers faster?" -- is genuinely open:** METR 2025 found experienced devs **19% slower** with AI, while a Google RCT (~21% faster) and a GitHub Copilot RCT (**55.8% faster** on greenfield) found gains. *(stated.)* A neutral study that can come back either way is exactly what a sophisticated buyer trusts.
- **Boudreau/Lacetera/Lakhani (2011, Management Science):** more competitors + higher problem uncertainty -> better contest outcomes -- academic support for a premium, hard-problem elite format. *(high.)*

## Beat 7 -- The one caveat to hold in mind (do not volunteer, but do not deny)

The willingness-to-pay for *our specific deliverable on this specific cohort* is not yet proven by any external number -- the category is real, the specific check is the thing the first paid pilot tests. Sell **depth** (observed causal behavior on an elite panel) against SlashData's **breadth** (12,500+-dev surveys); never oversell generalizability from n~200.

## Do NOT quote these (vendor marketing / contradicted / excluded)

These circulate online and sound useful. They failed verification -- do not put them in a deck or say them on a call:

- **"55% lower cost-per-hire" / "80% higher candidate satisfaction" / "4.2 qualified hires per event"** -- vendor-marketing, no disclosed methodology.
- **"$300B in global economic value from hackathons"** -- no methodology; inflated marketing copy.
- **Gartner "17% of buyer time with vendors" / "70-80% of journey before vendor contact"** -- could not be verified to a primary Gartner page. (The **61% rep-free** figure IS verified -- use that instead.)
- **A "$266.8M" price for the Zoom/Common Room deal** -- contradicted by primary sources; terms were undisclosed.
- **"40% of companies use hackathons for hiring"** -- vendor blog, unsourced.
- **AI Engineer "runs on ~9 FTEs / $9M+ revenue"** -- untraceable.
- **Any hackathon->startup or hackathon->hire *conversion rate*** -- no methodology-disclosed study exists.

When you need a number that isn't here, ask before improvising -- the whole point of this brief is that every figure on it is real.

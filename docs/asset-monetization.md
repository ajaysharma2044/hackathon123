# Monetizing the Non-Sponsor Assets

What the audience, the content, the data, and the credits are actually worth. Sourced.

## The finding that challenges a core assumption

**TreeHacks already flies people in, houses them, and feeds them.** In their own words: *"We
take care of meals, travels, and lodging so you can focus on building."* Scale: 1,000+ hackers,
30 universities, 12 countries, $150K+ in prizes.

The premium fly-in model is not the differentiator. It exists, at five times the planned scale,
at a school that is on the target recruiting list. Anyone pitched on "we fly in elite builders"
can reasonably answer "so does TreeHacks, and they fly in a thousand."

What remains genuinely differentiated:
- **Curation at 200** — a selected cohort, not a large open one
- **The research layer** — nobody in this category sells answers
- **Longitudinal outcome tracking** — nobody measures past Sunday night

The pitch has to rest entirely on those three. Flights and hotels are table stakes at the top of
this market, not the product.

## Do not sell impressions

A 200-person list is worth **$4–$15 per send** as advertising inventory. No amount of eliteness
changes that arithmetic.

| Basis | Value per send | Weekly for a year |
|---|---:|---:|
| TLDR's published $20 CPM | $4.00 | $208 |
| JavaScript Weekly $22 CPM | $4.42 | $230 |
| Postgres Weekly $74 CPM (most premium found) | $14.81 | $770 |
| Hypothetical 3x-elite premium | $44.40 | $2,309 |

Even at triple the most premium developer-media CPM in existence, a 200-person list is
~$2,300/year. The same 200 people framed as recruiting access — at standard agency economics of
20–25% of first-year salary — are worth **$60,000 for two hires** and **$250,000 for five at
$200K**. A ~100x difference, from framing alone.

### But the niche premium is real, and it's the whole argument

From Cooperpress's published Q3 2026 rate card:

| Newsletter | Subscribers | Price | CPM |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| JavaScript Weekly | 166,592 | $3,680 | $22.09 |
| **Postgres Weekly** | **17,282** | **$1,280** | **$74.07** |

Postgres Weekly has **10x fewer subscribers** and charges only **35% less** — a **3.4x CPM**.

> **Narrowness and seniority drive price, not size.** This is the empirical basis for the
> curation argument, and it comes from a publisher's own live rate card rather than theory.

## The benchmark that should set your price: PyCon US 2026

2,500+ attendees. Published tiers:

| Tier | Price | Per attendee |
|---|---:|---:|
| Visionary | $160,000 | $64.00 |
| Sustainability | $99,000 | $39.60 |
| Maintaining | $65,000 | $26.00 |
| Contributing | $33,500 | $13.40 |
| Supporting | $16,750 | $6.70 |

Named add-ons: **WiFi $8,000 · Lanyard $8,000 · Registration & Opening Reception $8,000 ·
Coffee Break $5,000 · Travel Grant Sponsor $2,500.** A conference pass is valued at $899.

**At PyCon's top rate of $64/attendee, a 200-person event commands $12,800.** To justify six
figures you must argue a **10–20x per-head premium → $128,000–$256,000**, earned with evidence
about who is in the room and what the sponsor walks away with. Impressions-based pricing cannot
get there; selection and deliverable can.

Note that **Travel Grant Sponsor** line. PyCon sells attendee travel funding as a named slot.
Your single largest cost is directly sponsorable as branded inventory.

## The most defensible media product: a sponsored content track

The Python Software Foundation prices this explicitly:

| | |
|---|---:|
| "Python and the Future of AI" track — **Lead Sponsor** | **$75,000** — *marked Filled* |
| Same track — Co-Sponsor (limit 2) | $50,000 |
| "Trailblazing Python Security" — Lead | $45,000 |
| Same — Co-Sponsor | $25,000 |

**$50–75K to put a name on a curated content stream**, and the AI slot sold out. This maps
almost exactly onto the sponsor-defined tracks already in the concept — meaning that model has a
published market price at a 2,500-person event, and yours is more curated.

This is a far better media product than a highlight reel. No hackathon or student builder event
was found to have sold media rights, licensed footage, or signed a documentary deal with
disclosed terms — price against content-track comparables, not imagined deals.

## Devpost is already doing a version of this

The most important competitive finding. **Devpost's "AI Trends Report 2026"** is hackathon
project data turned into AI-ecosystem market intelligence. Published findings include:

- *"GPT had 96% of the market in Q1 2023. Now, Claude is within a single point of it."*
  (GPT 96%→25%, Gemini 2%→47%, Claude <1%→20%)
- Enterprise AI hackathons grew 83% last year
- 1 in 4 community submissions is now agentic
- Developers with 6+ hackathons ship to production 2.2x more often than first-timers

Two things to learn from it:

**1. Copy their methodology disclosure.** Their charts are labeled **"Sponsor-adjusted: each
vendor's sponsored hackathons removed from that vendor's count."** They pre-empt the single most
obvious objection to research produced at a sponsored event — that sponsors buy their own
numbers. Any research product built on sponsorship lives or dies on that credibility, and the
format is already proven.

**2. They give it away.** The incumbent with the best hackathon dataset on earth uses the
research as **gated lead-gen** for enterprise hackathon services, complete with a press kit of
embeddable charts. That is a real strategic signal: the research may be worth more as a demand
generator than as a line item.

The gap Devpost leaves open is significant, though — theirs is submission-metadata analysis
across many events. It cannot see onboarding friction, tool switching, time-to-first-success, or
30-day retention, because it never instruments the building itself. That is exactly the space
the brokered-API-key instrumentation occupies.

## What research actually sells for

Gartner's **published** government net prices, per seat per year (discounted public-sector
rates; commercial list is typically higher):

| Seat | Price |
|---|---:|
| Global CIO Team Plus (invitation only) | $225,424 |
| **Software Engineering Leaders / CDAO / CISO** | **$128,352** |
| Self-Directed Team Leader | $73,481 |
| Advisor Member | $57,521 |
| Role Team Member | $19,638 |
| Essentials Team Member | $10,290 |

A single Software Engineering Leaders seat costs more than the target anchor sponsorship.

⚠️ **SlashData — the closest analog to this product — publishes no pricing at all.** Every one of
its six product lines is "contact us." Stack Overflow advertising is likewise "talk to an
expert." The category norm is opaque pricing, which is itself useful: it means there is no public
anchor a sponsor can hold you to.

## Credits are worth $0 to you

For 200 people in ~60 teams, face value looks enormous:

| Provider | Per team | 60 teams |
|---|---|---:|
| AWS Activate Founders | $1,000–$5,000 | $60K–$300K |
| Google Cloud (pre-funded) | $2,000 | $120,000 |
| Microsoft for Startups Founders Hub | up to $150,000 | — |

**Organizer-captured value: zero.** Credits provision into *participants'* accounts. They cost
the provider nearly nothing at the margin, which is exactly why they are easy to get and why
they must never be traded for anything scarce — attendee relationships, data access, or naming
rights.

> A "$500K in credits" headline is real as a recruiting and PR asset and worth nothing as
> income. The negotiation that matters is converting a credits-only sponsor into a **cash**
> sponsor, with credits as table stakes rather than the deal.

Note: AWS Activate's $200K Portfolio tier requires an **Organization ID from an Activate
Provider** (accelerator, angel, or VC) — a lever worth pulling if the organization can become a
recognized provider. Anthropic's startup program explicitly includes *"community access through
hackathons, Founder Days, and meetups"*; credit amounts are not disclosed, and no published
per-event hackathon credit program was found from Anthropic or OpenAI.

## Charging participants: real precedent, against the grain

Elite hackathons pay attendees rather than charging them (TreeHacks covers travel and lodging).
But premium builder gatherings do charge:

- **Edge Esmeralda 2026:** Standard Monthly **$2,400 → $3,000**, Weekly **$1,050 → $1,350**,
  prices escalating monthly, **lodging and meals not included**, explicitly framed around
  self-sustainability.
- **PyCon** values a pass at **$899**.

Elite builders will pay four figures when the selection bar is credible. ⚠️ No published
hackathon no-show statistics and no documented case of a refundable commitment deposit at an
elite builder event were found — adopting one means doing it without precedent to point to.

## Two transferable models

**buildspace** — the founder's shutdown letter (Aug 2024) states the company closed for personal
reasons, *not* financial ones: *"no, it isn't because of monetization (we'd confidently make
$3-5m arr off sponsorships to at least pay the bills)"* with *"over 2+ years in the bank."*
a16z-backed. **The strongest public evidence that builder-community sponsorship is a
multi-million-dollar business** — and a caution that it depended entirely on one founder's
motivation.

**Hack Club HCB** — fiscal sponsorship at *"one simple, transparent fee: 7% of revenue,"*
$100M+ processed, 6,500+ projects, operating since 2018. The cleanest precedent for monetizing
money *flowing through* a community rather than attention — and directly relevant to the
501(c)(3) question.

**ETHGlobal** sells outcomes, not eyeballs: 95+ events, 14,000+ projects, **$350M+ raised by
companies from their events**. That last number is what sponsors buy.

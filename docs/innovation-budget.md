# Innovation Budget

The budget line above research. This is where the largest numbers in all of this research live,
and it has its own vocabulary.

## Never say "sponsorship"

Every structure that successfully captures innovation budget uses different words:

```
affiliate membership  ·  corporate partner fee  ·  design partner program
research wallet  ·  challenge vehicle  ·  consortium membership
```

Plug and Play's own FAQ explains why the fee sits where it does: *"The fee guarantees that we
have C-level buy in and an SVP champion."*

> **Price for the champion, not the logo.**

## Steal this: the research wallet

**Stanford HAI publishes explicit corporate tiers**, and the mechanic inside them is the single
most useful structural idea found in this research.

| Tier | Price | What's inside |
|---|---:|---|
| **Founding Member** | **$5,000,000/yr**, 3-year term | **$800K research wallet** spendable on research tokens and affiliate memberships across campus, $50K education wallet, three seats in each Exec Ed program, SAIL + Digital Economy Lab membership, visiting scholars |
| **Corporate Affiliate** | **$1,000,000**, 1-year term | **$400K research wallet**, one Exec Ed seat per program, Digital Economy Lab membership, SAIL with an $85K research token, **two visiting scholars sent to Stanford**, policy access |

The $400K isn't a fee — it's a **directed wallet the sponsor spends on projects they choose.**

That's the conversion you want. It turns a sponsorship into an **R&D line item with an owner and
a spend plan**. A sponsor can defend "a $250K partnership, $100K of which is a directed build
wallet across teams we select" to a finance committee. They cannot defend a gold tier.

Directly applicable: sponsors pick which builder teams their wallet funds, which also makes them
care about the outcomes — and gives you the follow-on R&D product from
[products.md](products.md) without a separate sale.

## University affiliate comps

| | Price |
|---|---:|
| **MIT Media Lab** — implied average per member | **~$560,000/yr** (80+ members, ~$45M operating budget) |
| MIT Media Lab — Consortium Research Sponsor (historical) | $400,000/yr |
| MIT Media Lab — "Things That Think" (historical) | $200,000/yr min, 3 years |
| **Stanford Social Algorithms Lab** | **$75,000/yr** (+$12,000/quarter to embed a visitor) |
| **Stanford Digital Economy Lab** | **$50,000/yr** |
| UW CSE Industry Affiliates (large companies) | $10,000/yr |
| UW-Madison Statistics (>$1M revenue) | $10,000/yr |

**UC Berkeley prices the structural distinction explicitly:** consortium and affiliate agreements
carry **20% overhead versus the standard 52% research rate.** Universities price "buy access" far
below "buy research" — which is another argument for selling the research, not the access.

## ⚠️ Correction: the Stanford Computer Forum figure

I used **$27,000/year for Stanford Computer Forum** as a central argument — the ceiling that
proves elite student access is cheap. **That number could not be independently verified.** Their
join and benefits pages are JavaScript-rendered and publish no amount.

The verified Stanford comps are **$75,000 (Social Algorithms Lab)** and **$50,000 (Digital
Economy Lab)** — both higher, and both for research affiliation rather than recruiting access.

**The student-access-trap argument still holds**, because the **CMU Student Career Sponsor at
$10,000 for two semesters** came from a live published page. But treat the Stanford $27K as
unconfirmed and don't put it in a deck.

|---:|---|---|

| **$1,269,946** | DoD | **Accenture Federal — DLA R&D Generative AI Hackathon** |
| $810,945 | DoD | Knexus Research — hackathon task order |
| **$299,992** | DoD | Emerge Americas Hackathon (USF) |
| $239,652 | State Dept | CodeImpact US–Nepal Hackathon **Series** |
| $67,502 | USAID | APEC hackathon — **logistics and planning only** |
| $28,675 | VA | "Hackathon event for innovation" |

```
$28K – $68K       logistics only
$240K – $300K     branded multi-event series
$600K – $2.5M     enterprise hackathon with real delivery attached
```

**The Accenture/DLA generative-AI hackathon at $1.27M is the single best comp** for "a large
organization paid an outside firm to run a build event." It proves the ceiling exists when actual
output is attached to the event.

## Innovation consulting — what IDEO actually bills

No design firm publishes rates, but IDEO's federal contracts are public:

| Amount | Agency |
|---:|---|
| **$2,973,744** | HHS — design and pilot new dissemination strategies |
| $1,680,606 | State Dept — systems design |
| $1,405,694 | DoD — neuroscience engagements, Biotechnology Office |
| $1,146,940 | CFPB — consumer experience strategy |
| $739,578 | CFPB — digital tool + design transition |

**IDEO engagements cluster $0.5M–$3M**, and government rates typically run *below* commercial.

BCG Digital Ventures / BCG X publishes nothing; their model is consulting fees plus joint-venture
agreements plus **equity stakes in co-created businesses**.

## Prize challenges are a $2.8M–$15M business

**Luminary Labs** is the purest comparable — a firm paid to design and run prize challenges:

| Amount | Agency | Scope |
|---:|---|---|
| **$100,000,000** | HHS/BARDA | "Other Transaction Prize Vehicle" (ceiling) |
| $15,390,701 | NASA | NOIS2 MagQuest Phase 4 |
| $7,302,621 | NASA | TechLeap Prize 2026 |
| $4,999,012 | Dept of Ed | Plan + implement CTE Challenge |

**XPRIZE's own 990 reveals the ratio that matters: operating costs run roughly as large as the
prize purses.** Running the prize costs about what the prize pays. Buyers accept roughly 20–100%
overhead on top of prize money for someone to run it properly — HeroX's reported 18%-of-purse
success fee points the same way.

Also useful at the small end: Topcoder was paid **$100,000 for a single NASA "Image Based Crater
Detection" challenge.** ETHGlobal-style individual sponsor tracks run **$10K–$20K each** (ENS
$20K; Sui, Worldcoin, 0G $15K; Uniswap Foundation $10K) — so a bounty track buy-in is effectively
$10–20K.

## The corporate partner benchmark

**MassChallenge** takes nothing from startups — no fee, no equity — so essentially all revenue is
corporate partners and philanthropy. From their Form 990s:

| FY | Revenue | Contributions |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2023 | $10.29M | $9.96M |
| 2021 | $12.99M | $12.49M |
| 2019 | $14.29M | $13.16M |

Against a partner roster in the 40–60 range, that implies roughly **$150,000–$300,000 per
corporate partner per year.** (Derived, not published.)

## ⚠️ A fabricated source to avoid

The widely-circulating Techstars corporate-accelerator figures — "$500K–$2M," with case studies
of "$1.2M pharma / $750K fintech / $600K CPG" — trace to `startuplab.today`, **a domain that does
not resolve.** It is AI-generated SEO content.

**Do not put those numbers in a deck.** Techstars and Plug and Play corporate fees are genuinely
private; the reliable path is asking a corporate innovation lead who has bought one.

## The band this supports

```
$150,000 – $300,000 / year      corporate partner / affiliate membership
                                triangulated from MassChallenge per-partner economics,
                                MIT Media Lab's ~$560K average, and the $240-300K federal
                                band for a branded hackathon series

$600,000 – $2,500,000           only with real delivery attached
                                (Accenture/DLA proves this ceiling is real)
```

That is materially above the [the-quote.md](the-quote.md) band of $75–250K, because it is a
different product: **an annual partnership with a directed wallet**, not a single study.

## One more unit-economics line for the pitch

**User Interviews charges $98 for one hour of one qualified B2B person's attention.**

Two hundred elite builders for two days is a different order of magnitude entirely — and pure
recruiting alone at that published rate is **~$19,600 for 200 one-hour sessions**, before
incentives, before analysis, before anyone builds anything.

Lead with that arithmetic. It does the selling.

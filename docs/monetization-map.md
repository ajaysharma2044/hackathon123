# Monetization Map — Every Step

The creative brief: monetize every aspect. The discipline: tag each honestly, and measure each
against the one constraint that governs everything.

**Tags:** `[PROVEN]` someone verifiably pays for this · `[PLAUSIBLE]` strong analog, unproven for
us · `[SWING]` creative, no evidence yet · `⚠️GOOSE` risks the builder experience or hits a legal
wall (see [recruiting-legal.md](recruiting-legal.md), [venture-upside.md](venture-upside.md)).

## The governing constraint: the extraction frontier

```
INVISIBLE to builder ──────────────────────────────── VISIBLE to builder
IMPROVES their weekend ───────────────────────────── TAXES their weekend
   ↑                                                        ↑
   safe to monetize hard                        monetize only if it adds value
   (telemetry, logistics, B2B research)          (or don't — the panel is worth more)
```

Every idea below is placed on this frontier. The ones on the left are free money. The ones on the
right are where most organizers kill their own event. **The whole game is loading revenue onto the
left side and keeping the right side feeling like a gift.**

## Phase 0 — The application funnel (before anyone is selected)

The 3,000 applicants are an asset *before* the 200 are chosen. Almost nobody monetizes this.

- **The application prompt is a research instrument.** `[SWING]` A sponsor pays to have the
  application question be *theirs* — "design a payment flow for X" — and gets 3,000 elite students'
  approaches to their problem, for free, months before the event. Early-funnel idea-corpus. Invisible to the builder (it just feels like a good application). **Left side.**
- **Sponsor-funded seats / scholarships.** `[PROVEN]` Reality Hack: $5,000 = travel + housing for
  4 builders. Named, attributable, draws from brand/CSR budget. Improves the weekend. **Left side.**
- **The rejected 2,800 are still elite.** `[PLAUSIBLE] ⚠️GOOSE` A warm, consented second-tier talent
  pool — but FCRA and consent make this delicate; only with explicit opt-in, never a score.
- **Application data as a leading indicator.** `[SWING]` "What are the top CS students building
  and reaching for right now" — aggregate trend signal, sold as intelligence. Consent-gated.

## Phase 1 — Selection

- **Sponsors buy a question on the intake.** `[PLAUSIBLE]` The omnibus model (SSRS/Researchscape
  charge $1,000/question, confidential per client) applied at the funnel: one application, several
  sponsors' proprietary screening questions. Left side.
- **⚠️ Do NOT sell a "who's best" score.** `⚠️GOOSE` The instant you emit a ranked employability
  number you're a regulated assessment tool (NYC LL144, EU AI Act). Sell evidence, never a score.

## Phase 2 — Travel & arrival

- **Airline travel sponsorship.** `[PROVEN]` Delta gave $3,600 in flight credits to a Miami
  hackathon. Turns your largest cost into a sponsor's brand moment. Left side.
- **Group-travel commissions.** `[PROVEN]` IATA TIDS + room-block commissions + rebates =
  ~$20-26K recovered on 600 room nights. Fully invisible to the builder. **Pure left side.**
- **The arrival itself as onboarding data.** `[SWING]` First-touch survey, baseline stack, intent.

## Phase 3 — The event (the dense core)

**Left side — extract hard, builder never notices:**
- **Brokered API keys** `[PLAUSIBLE]` — per-participant credentials = server-side truth on
  activation, errors, switching, abandonment. The core research instrument. A term of the
  research tier.
- **The unconstrained tool-choice baseline** `[PROVEN as valuable]` — what 200 elite builders reach
  for when nothing's required. Established as the single most valuable data point; sold aggregate.
- **Meal / break / keycard / lanyard / wifi sponsorship** `[PROVEN]` — $2-15K each; the room block
  is itself ad inventory a commuter event can't sell.

**Value-add side — monetized because it IMPROVES the weekend:**
- **Sponsor mentors embedded as engineers** `[PLAUSIBLE]` — sponsors PAY to put staff in the room
  (it's their recruiting + research + design-partner channel), and builders *want* expert help.
  Both sides win. The mentor's office-hours questions are also data.
- **Sponsor-defined bounty tracks** `[PROVEN]` — ETHGlobal sells individual sponsor tracks at
  $10-20K each; the builder experiences it as "cool prize," the sponsor gets directed building.
- **Live product A/B tests** `[PLAUSIBLE]` — which onboarding flow, which docs, which starter
  template. Free randomized experiments inside a non-randomized event. Builder just sees a tool.
- **The API stress-test / red-team reframe** `[SWING]` — 200 elite devs hammering your API for 48
  hours IS a load and DX stress test. Companies pay pentest and DX firms far more. "Your API,
  tested to destruction by 200 builders in a weekend."
- **Workshops as paid slots** `[PROVEN]` — PSF sells a content track at $75K; a workshop is the
  builder-useful version.

## Phase 4 — Judging & demo day

- **VC demo-day access** `[PROVEN]` — VCs pay $2-5K per qualified referral (Republic/Calm Company);
  a demo day is a deal-flow event. Sell observer access to VCs who didn't sponsor a track.
- **Design-partner pipeline** `[SWING]` — teams that built on a sponsor's API and loved it = warm,
  qualified design-partner intros. Design partners are gold in B2B; sponsors pay real money to
  assemble a cohort. This is a *better* recruiting-adjacent product than placement.
- **First-look / IP** `[PLAUSIBLE] ⚠️GOOSE` — sponsors get early look at what was built on their
  platform. Clean as a look; poisonous if it becomes an equity or IP grab (see venture-upside.md).
- **Recorded demos = branded content** `[PLAUSIBLE]` — buildspace turned this energy into ~$3-5M
  ARR of sponsorship. Developer-marketing gold, and builders love the spotlight. Value-add.

## Phase 5 — Post-event (the moat)

- **7/30/90-day outcome tracking** `[PLAUSIBLE]` — the retention data no other hackathon produces.
  The heart of the research deliverable.
- **Continuation capital** `[PROVEN structures]` — teams that keep going → post-hoc SPV
  (Sydecar ~$4,500) or VC referral memo ($5K each). Never a participation-equity grab.
- **The alumni network as recurring community** `[PLAUSIBLE]` — and next year's warm applicant pool.

## The two ideas that might be bigger than the event

**1. The between-events research panel.** `[SWING — but this is the big one]`
The event's real output may not be the weekend — it's a **vetted, consented, profiled panel of
elite builders who earn money doing async research all year.** Companies pay $200-500 per
hard-to-reach technical respondent; this panel is unbuyable elsewhere. **The event becomes the
recruiting cost for a year-round research-panel business.** The hackathon is the customer-
acquisition funnel; the panel is the recurring-revenue engine. Wynter ($20-100K/yr subscriptions)
and RippleMatch ($68K avg contract) prove the recurring shape.

**2. The methodology as a platform.** `[SWING]`
Once the playbook works, other universities/organizers run it under license and you take a cut —
Hack Club's 7%-of-revenue fiscal-sponsorship model scaled them from $1.6M to $22.4M in five years.
The event is one node; the network of events is the business.

## The wildest swings (low confidence, high ceiling)

- **Builder-behavior data as an AI training/eval set** `[SWING] ⚠️GOOSE` — Stack Overflow now
  licenses its corpus to AI companies. How elite builders actually use tools, with consent, could
  be an eval dataset. Consent-heavy, category is real, ceiling is high.
- **The "experimentation marketplace"** `[SWING]` — companies submit a question, you route it to
  the right builders in the right format (event / sprint / async). The event is one delivery
  format of a general decision-intelligence network.
- **Category intelligence as a subscription** `[SWING]` — across many events, the switching matrix
  and tool-choice trends become a longitudinal data product nobody else has.
- **"Options on talent"** `[SWING] ⚠️GOOSE` — not placement fees (legally fraught), but sponsors
  paying for an early, structured relationship with builders. Reframe carefully around the
  employment-agency and FCRA lines.

## What this actually says

You can, in principle, monetize ~25 distinct nodes. **You should not monetize all of them** — the
right side of the frontier is where events eat their own seed corn. The disciplined read:

1. **Load revenue on the left side** (telemetry, logistics, aggregate research) — invisible, safe,
   and most of it is `[PROVEN]` or `[PLAUSIBLE]`.
2. **On the right side, only monetize what adds value** — mentors, bounties, spotlight, capital.
3. **The single highest-ceiling idea isn't a step in the event at all** — it's the year-round panel
   the event recruits. Test that hypothesis alongside the event thesis.

**Honest status:** almost everything above is `[SWING]` or `[PLAUSIBLE]`. Two nodes are genuinely
`[PROVEN]` at meaningful scale (logistics recovery, sponsor-funded travel/bounties). The map is a
menu of hypotheses to test, not a revenue forecast — exactly one of them (will a buyer pay for the
core research?) still gates all the others, per [STATE.md](STATE.md).

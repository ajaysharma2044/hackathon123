## The two numbers that define the problem

### 1. Per-attendee spend

```
HackMIT / PennApps      ~$200–240 per attendee
This event (~$280K/200)  ~$1,400 per attendee        ← 6–7x the industry norm
```

Because normal hackathons don't fly people in or put them in hotels — attendees drive
themselves and sleep on the floor. Flights and housing are the entire difference.

**Implication:** the per-attendee revenue has to be 6–7x better too. There is no version of
this that works on standard hackathon sponsorship economics.

### 2. You cannot win on attention

Cal Hacks has **3,000+ hackers** and its top published tier is **$50K**.
This event has **200**. Fifteen times fewer people.

On an attention basis — eyeballs, booth traffic, resume volume — a 200-person event is worth a
*fraction* of Cal Hacks, not a multiple. Any sponsor comparing the two on those terms will price
you at $5–10K, and they will be right to.

> **The only justification for charging more per sponsor than the world's largest collegiate
> hackathon is selling something it does not sell.** That is the entire strategic argument for
> the research product, and it is now empirically grounded rather than aspirational.

The good news: Cal Hacks' own packet proves nobody in this market sells answers. The category is
empty.

### 3. Seventy sponsors is not available to you

HackMIT needed **70+ sponsors and 20 organizers** to raise $250K. That is ~$3.5K per sponsor and
a sales operation of twenty people.

Two people cannot run a 70-account pipeline. The arithmetic forces the strategy:

```
70 sponsors × $3.5K     = $245K     needs 20 organizers      ✗ not available
11 sponsors × ~$25K avg = $280K     needs a differentiated product   ← the only path
```

Fewer, larger sponsors isn't merely preferable. For a two-person team it is the only
operationally feasible structure.

## Structural finding: Cal Hacks is a 501(c)(3)

> "Payments are donations to the Cal Hacks Foundation, a registered 501(c)(3) non-profit."

This is worth serious thought. Nonprofit status:

**Helps**
- Sponsor payments become tax-deductible charitable donations
- Opens budget paths beyond marketing — community, DEI, charitable giving
- Unlocks foundation grants a for-profit cannot receive (Knight Foundation funds Miami tech
  ecosystem heavily — under research)
- Solves procurement: large companies find it far easier to donate to a registered nonprofit
  than to cut a check to two individuals
- Liability and contract-holding entity

**Blocks**
- Taking equity in participant startups
- Recruiting placement/success fees
- Running the research/data business as a profit-making enterprise
- Distributing profit to founders

**Likely resolution: two entities.** A 501(c)(3) that runs the event and receives sponsor
donations, and a C-corp that owns the platform, the dataset, and the research product, with a
documented arm's-length services agreement between them. This is a common structure but it
needs a real lawyer — related-party transactions between a nonprofit and a founder-owned
for-profit are exactly what the IRS scrutinizes (private benefit / inurement).

Flagging early because it affects entity formation, which is on the 30-day list and blocks
contract signing.

## Miami-specific

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Hotel seasonality** | August ~**$200/night** average vs February ~**$455/night**. Summer (Jun–Sep) is the softest rate period of the year; winter (Dec–Apr) is peak. ([source](https://www.jetsetteralerts.com/the-cheapest-and-most-expensive-times-to-visit-miami/), [source](https://trip-signal.com/blog/best-time-to-book-hotels-in-miami)) |
| **Hurricane cancellation** | VISIT FLORIDA **"Cover Your Event"** — a *free* program reimbursing rescheduling costs and room-rate differences if a named hurricane cancels the event. Sublimits **$100K–$200K** scaled to contracted room nights. Limited availability, subject to approval. ([source](https://www.visitflorida.com/meeting-planners/cover-your-event-insurance/)) |
| **eMerge Americas 2027** | **March 2–4, 2027**, Miami. 20,000+ attendees, 400+ companies, backed by City of Miami Beach. Sponsors are already in town with Miami budget allocated that week. ([source](https://emergeamericas.com/)) |

### The Miami date tradeoff

```
AUGUST          hotels ~$200/night, cheapest of the year
                peak hurricane season, brutal heat
                free VISIT FLORIDA hurricane coverage available
                students between internship and semester

MARCH (eMerge)  hotels ~$455/night — 2.3x more expensive
                sponsors already in Miami with allocated budget
                no hurricane risk
                mid-semester: students must miss class
```

On 600 room nights that rate difference is roughly **$150K** — larger than most of the
sponsorship stack. August is the financially rational choice, and the free hurricane program is
what makes the risk carryable.

The eMerge-adjacency argument is real but should be tested as a *sponsor-acquisition* trip
rather than an event date: go to eMerge in March 2027 to sell, run the event in August.

| Full-service agency research study | **$15K–$40K** | same |
| B2B research participant cost | **$150–$250 fully loaded per participant** | same |

### What that last row means

At $150–250 fully loaded per B2B research participant, **200 consented technical participants is
$30–50K of raw panel value before a single question is asked** — and elite engineers are among
the hardest and most expensive segments for panel companies to recruit at all.

That is a floor, not a ceiling, and it is a number a research buyer already understands.

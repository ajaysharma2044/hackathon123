# What These Firms Pay For Research — Verified

Evidence, not conclusion. This answers one question directly: **do the target companies actually
spend money on external research, right now?** Yes — verified across 13+ recurring programs with
named vendors. It does **not** prove they will pay *us* (see the caveat at the bottom); it proves
the budget and the appetite are real and continuous.

## The samples are large and expensive

These cost real money to field — six figures each at the top:

- **Adyen:** 41,089 consumers + 14,003 merchants across 28 countries (2025)
- **Checkout.com:** 18,000+ consumers, 16 countries
- **Snowflake:** 2,050 respondents (from 3,479 screened)
- **Square:** 6,000 business owners + 4,000 consumers
- **Marqeta:** 5,000 consumers and SMBs

At hard-to-reach B2B rates ($40–500/complete), a 500-exec study alone is $20–250K of fieldwork
before analysis. Alloy runs one every year.

## The gap — and this is the actual finding

Across **every** verified program, the sample is one of three things, never independent
early-stage builders:

```
(a) The company's own customers / telemetry   Databricks (20,000 orgs), Snowflake ("generated
                                              revenue for Snowflake"), Ramp/Brex card data
(b) Enterprise execs or revenue-bearing SMBs  Alloy (fraud leaders at banks), Modern Treasury
                                              (500-9,999 employees), Fivetran (2,000+ employees)
    General-population consumers               Adyen, Checkout, Marqeta, Plaid, Unit
```

Three findings are quotable and verified:

1. **Square's 2025 methodology screens category (c) out in writing:** respondents' companies "had
   to have at least one employee and have revenue above $20,000."
2. **Alloy's frame structurally excludes it:** you need a compliance function to qualify.
3. **Snowflake's one startup-focused report interviews 8 venture capitalists about startups** —
   rather than asking a single founder anything. Even when the camera points at builders, it
   films the investors.

**Nobody in this set has ever asked an independent, pre-launch builder a question.** The budget
exists, the vendors exist, the annual cadence exists — and the cohort we'd uniquely have is the
one nobody samples.

⚠️ **Honest framing** (verified, so it survives scrutiny): don't claim "nobody researches early
builders" — Temporal (no size floor, 22% at 51–250 employees) and Airbyte (community-recruited,
one edition then abandoned) are exposure. The defensible claim is: *"the entire category samples
installed bases and enterprise purchase-influencers; the two largest reports are literally
customer telemetry; the survey-based ones screen for 500–2,000+ employees; and the one report
aimed at startups interviews VCs instead of founders."*

## The caveat that keeps this honest

Everything above proves these firms **buy research**. **None of it proves they would buy research
built on 200 elite _student_ builders at our price.** That is still the one untested unknown in
[STATE.md](STATE.md) — this evidence raises the prior that a buyer exists; it does not replace the
paid-pilot test that would confirm it. A firm paying Harris Poll to survey 500 fraud executives
has told you nothing about whether it values a study on student hackathon participants.

Two live doors this evidence surfaced, both verified:
- **DORA (Google Cloud) states publicly: "Sponsorship opportunities are available for upcoming
  DORA reports."**
- **MLH sells "product feedback and insights" to Google, Meta, GitHub, MongoDB — and has zero
  payments/fintech sponsors.** MongoDB proves the channel works; the fintech budget is unclaimed.

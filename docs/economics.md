# The Economic Surface

Most hackathon organizers monetize exactly one thing: sponsor logos. A 200-person flown-in
event generates at least eight distinct economic surfaces, and the other seven are where the
margin is.

> Research in progress — see [research-findings.md](research-findings.md) for sourced numbers.
> This document is the map; that one is the territory.

## The core reframe: four things you can sell

Every sponsor dollar buys one of four things. They are priced roughly an order of magnitude
apart, and most hackathons only ever sell the cheapest one.

| | What the sponsor gets | Market price | Who buys |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Attention** | Logo, booth, swag, workshop slot | $500 – $10K | Anyone |
| **Access** | Recruiting, resume book, candidate contact | $20K – $50K | ⚠️ *Not* university recruiting — NACE median campus budget is ~$2,850/school. Field Marketing holds this. See [procurement.md](procurement.md) |
| **Answers** | A research study answering their question | $40K – $150K | Product / DevRel / insights |
| **Outcomes** | A hire, a retained user, a portfolio company | Contingent, unbounded | All of the above |

**The same event sells all four at once, to different sponsors.** This is the single most
important structural point. Standard hackathon prospectuses collapse everything into one
attention ladder and cap out around $10K per sponsor — which is why they need 30 sponsors and
still don't clear six figures.

Confirmed benchmark: typical university hackathon tiers run Bronze $500 / Silver $1,000 /
Gold $2,500 / Platinum $5,000 / Diamond $10,000. That is the price of attention. Pricing
anywhere near it means the research thesis was never communicated.

## Layer 1 — Sponsorship, restructured

### Sell syndicated research, not just custom studies

The highest-margin structure in the entire research industry, and it applies directly here.

**Custom:** one sponsor pays $100K for a study answering their question.
**Syndicated:** five non-competing sponsors each pay $30K for a shared baseline study — "what
do 200 elite student builders actually choose, and why" — plus 2–3 proprietary questions each.

```
Custom      1 × $100K  = $100K    one buyer, one question, all the risk
Syndicated  5 × $30K   = $150K    five buyers, shared cost, better baseline
```

The syndicated version produces a *better* report — a shared baseline across five product
categories is more informative than one vendor's funnel in isolation — and it de-risks the
business, because losing one sponsor costs 20% of revenue instead of 100%. This is how
Gartner, Forrester, and SlashData actually operate.

### Sell the subscription, not the event

Per-event sponsorship means re-selling eleven companies every single year forever. An annual
research partnership — continuous experiments across events, quarterly category intelligence,
longitudinal panel access — converts that treadmill into recurring revenue and is worth
materially more per account.

Year one sells events. Year two should sell subscriptions with events attached.

## Layer 2 — The logistics layer: costs with revenue inside them

**Reframe: 600 hotel room nights is not a cost you pay. It is a product you sell to Miami.**

Cities and convention bureaus compete for room nights and have real budgets to win them. The
same is true of hotels, venues, and airlines. Every line in the cost model has a counterparty
who wants the business.

- **Room block commissions and comp rooms** — standard in the events industry, on 600 room
  nights this is not trivial
- **Destination marketing incentives** — Greater Miami CVB, Miami-Dade, Venture Miami
- **VISIT FLORIDA "Cover Your Event"** — *free* named-hurricane cancellation coverage with
  $100K–$200K sublimits scaled to contracted room nights. This is the thing that makes a
  cheap-season Miami event financially survivable.
- **F&B minimums against venue rental** — often the venue is free if catering clears a number
- **Airline group programs** — American hubs in Miami
- **Food and beverage brands** — they pay to feed developers

Numbers landing in [research-findings.md](research-findings.md).

## Layer 3 — Talent, beyond the flat fee

A flat recruiting sponsorship is a one-time payment for a permanent asset. The value delivered
is unbounded; the price is not.

Confirmed benchmarks: campus recruiting averages **~$6,275 cost-per-hire** (NACE), while agency
placement runs **15–30% of salary — $18K–$36K** on a $120K engineer. The spread between those
two numbers is the entire pricing opportunity, and it is much larger than any sponsorship tier.

Structures worth pricing:
- Flat recruiting access (safe, standard)
- Success fee on hires sourced from the event (aligned, legally complex — licensing rules apply)
- Ongoing talent marketplace subscription (recurring)

Legal constraints being researched. The line between "presenting work evidence" and "operating
an employment agency" is real, as is the line into regulated automated employment decision
tools.

## Layer 4 — The durable assets

The event ends Sunday. These don't.

- **The list.** A curated, verified roster of elite technical students is a standing asset.
  Developer-audience newsletters monetize at high CPMs; this audience is better than most.
- **The panel.** 200 consented, profiled, longitudinally tracked builders is a research asset
  that companies pay per-participant rates to reach — and reaching elite engineers is the
  hardest recruiting problem in research panels.
- **The dataset.** Compounds across events. Year three is worth far more than year one.
- **The methodology.** Licensable to other organizers.
- **The brand.** An annual "State of Student Builders" report, given away free, is a media
  property that generates inbound sponsor demand instead of outbound sales.

## Layer 5 — Venture upside

VCs pay for deal flow. Teams form at the event; some become companies. Structures range from
plain VC sponsorship (clean) through scout arrangements to equity or rights of first refusal in
teams formed on site (lucrative, conflict-heavy, potentially poisonous to participant trust).

Being researched, including whether taking equity in participants' companies while
simultaneously selling sponsors research about those same participants is a conflict that can
be disclosed away or one that has to be structurally avoided.

## Layer 6 — In-kind is real money

Cloud and AI credits have negligible marginal cost to the provider and real face value to you.
They fund the entire prize pool without touching cash, and a prize pool funded in credits looks
identical to participants. Same for hardware, swag, and sponsor-hosted venue space.

Every dollar of in-kind is a dollar you don't have to raise.

## The practical trap: working capital

Sponsors pay on net-30 to net-60, often *after* the event. Hotels and venues want deposits
months *before* it. A fully-funded event can still fail on timing.

Consequences for how deals are structured:
- Ask for 50% on signature, 50% net-30 post-event
- Sequence irreversible commitments against cash actually received, not committed
- The go/no-go date is about cash in the bank, not LOIs

## What this changes about the pitch

The sponsor conversation is not "here are our tiers." It is:

> What decision are you trying to make, and which of these four things — attention, access,
> answers, or outcomes — actually moves it?

Then price against *that* market. A recruiting buyer compares you to $18–36K agency fees. A
research buyer compares you to a $15–40K agency study or a Gartner engagement. Neither of them
compares you to a $10K Diamond hackathon tier unless you invite them to.
